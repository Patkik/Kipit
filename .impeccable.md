# Design Context

Confirmed by the user on 29 October 2026 during the Kipit redesign profiling conversation.

## Users

Personal spenders, students managing an allowance, and households managing money. The shared entry point must remain understandable without finance expertise. Serve this range through progressive disclosure rather than showing all features on the home screen. Household support here describes the intended audience, not a claim of shared-account or multi-user functionality.

## Primary task

Immediately understand how much can be spent today. Expense entry is the main supporting action. Wallet balances, budgets, history and insights are secondary destinations.

## Brand personality

Calm, practical, clear. Crisp controls, distinct sections and restrained forest accents. The user explicitly rejects text-heavy screens, confusing choices, plain-text-looking action controls and generic AI aesthetics.

## Aesthetic direction

Native Android with readable money amounts, visible control boundaries, purposeful icons with short labels, subtle surface grouping and deliberate spacing. Keep light and dark themes and offline operation. Avoid decoration that competes with the allowance or makes navigation ambiguous. The existing project rule to use bundled system typography takes precedence over general skill suggestions to browse fonts.

## Design principles

1. Answer today's spending question before showing other content.
2. Give each screen one clear purpose; move configuration out of everyday balance views.
3. Group by task with proximity, dividers and meaningful surfaces; avoid nested cards and identical tile grids.
4. Make important actions visibly interactive. Keep short labels beside icons; do not replace labels with mystery icons.
5. Reveal explanations and advanced controls only where needed, while keeping amounts, fees, errors and confirmation consequences visible.
6. Preserve financial precision and review boundaries; visual redesign does not authorize silent accounting changes.
